# Editing-Style Prompt: Cinematic Medical-Story Reel (9:16)

Use this prompt to edit **my own footage** in the style of the reference video. The reference
is only a guide to the editing style. Don't copy its content, shots or length. Apply the style
to whatever clips I provide.

---

## 1. Master editing prompt

> Edit my footage into a **vertical 9:16 (1080×1920), 24 fps** cinematic medical-storytelling
> reel. The style is a doctor telling a real patient case like a suspense story: talking-head
> segments of me cut against cinematic B-roll that acts out the story.
>
> **Pacing:** fast and story-driven. Hard cuts on the beat of the voiceover. Most B-roll
> shots are 1.5–4 seconds, and talking-head segments run 3–7 seconds. Cut away from me as soon
> as the story moves to a visual moment, and cut back to me for the hook, the key fact and the
> final cliffhanger. Never leave a shot static. Every clip has slow movement.
>
> **Look:** warm, filmic teal-and-orange grade, protected skin tones, shallow depth of field,
> slightly lifted blacks, 5% film grain. Patient/hospital shots are graded cooler and 10% less
> saturated so they feel clinical and tense.
>
> **Sound:** voiceover first. Low tension/ambient music bed well under the voice. Sound
> design on every visual action (whooshes on transitions, sizzles, mechanical hits, breathing,
> heartbeat or drone under tense moments).
>
> **Captions:** word-by-word burned-in captions that **never cover my face** (rules in §4).

---

## 2. Story structure (applies to any case I send)

1. **Hook on me:** I say the patient's opening line or symptom. Show it as a large quote
   pop-up in quotation marks.
2. **The patient now:** cut quickly to the patient in distress (hospital bed, oxygen,
   struggling), cooler grade.
3. **Rewind / backstory:** B-roll of what the patient was doing before, with lots of
   texture: close-ups of hands, tools, objects, faces reacting.
4. **Clues:** product shots, screenshots, close-ups of the suspicious detail, each with a
   slow push-in or slide.
5. **Back to me for the key fact:** a sudden zoom-in on the line that explains the mechanism.
6. **The reveal / cliffhanger:** end on the culprit object or a puzzled reaction, with a
   pointer line connecting the person to the object. Hard cut out.

---

## 3. Motion graphics and transitions

1. **Constant drift on every clip:** a 3–8% scale or position keyframe across each shot (slow
   push-in, lateral slide or tilt). Nothing sits completely still.
2. **Sudden zoom-ins on me:** on emphasis words, cut with an instant zoom from 100% to 112–115%
   (no ease). Return to the wider framing on the next talking-head cut.
3. **Quote pop-up (hook):** the patient's line in quotation marks, e.g. `"I CAN'T BREATHE"`.
   Font: Bebas Neue / Anton / Oswald Bold, white, 3 px black stroke, soft 40% shadow. Animation:
   scale 80%→100% + fade-in over 3 frames, ease-out. Position: **on my chest, below the chin.**
4. **Colored smoke / fumes VFX:** for any toxic or chemical moment, add a stock smoke element
   tinted cyan `#2FD3E0` (Screen blend, ~70%) rising from the bottom of the frame, plus orange
   `#FF9A3C` spark particles with motion blur.
5. **Flash and glow on bright moments** (sparks, fire, lights, impacts): bloom/glow (cyan or
   warm tint), 2 white flash frames at the impact, and a 4–6 px camera shake for ~8 frames.
6. **Screenshot / product inserts:** a phone or web screenshot fills the frame on white.
   It slides in from the right (8 frames, ease-out), then slowly zooms toward the important
   detail (price, label, ingredient). Optional red hand-drawn circle around the key detail
   (draws itself in over ~10 frames).
7. **Rack focus and foreground parallax:** where the footage allows, pull focus from object to
   face, or slide past a foreground object to create depth.
8. **Short motion-blur moves** between related action shots. Otherwise use clean hard cuts.
   No fancy transitions such as spins, glitches or page curls.
9. **Pointer line (ending):** a thin 3 px red `#E53935` line draws itself from the person to the
   culprit object over 6 frames.
10. **Short slow-motion** (60 fps conformed to 24) for reveal moments, such as lifting a mask or
    reacting to a smell.

---

## 4. Captions that **never cover my face** (9:16)

- **Style:** Montserrat ExtraBold or Bebas Neue, 2–4 words per line, one line at a time,
  white with a 3 px black stroke and a light shadow. Highlight the key word of each sentence
  (symptom, diagnosis, culprit) in yellow `#FFD400`.
- **Animation:** each word pops in on its spoken syllable (scale 90%→100%, 2 frames).
- **Framing for my talking-head shots:** frame me so my **eyes sit at about 30–35% of frame
  height** and my face stays in the **top ~45%** of the frame. This leaves my chest free for text.
- **Caption position:**
  - **Talking-head shots:** caption centered at **60–68% of frame height**, on my chest/torso,
    clearly below my chin. If my chin drops lower in a take, move the caption lower. Never
    place text anywhere between my eyebrows and my chin.
  - **B-roll shots:** same 60–68% zone by default. If a face or the key action is in that zone,
    move the caption to the **upper third (~20–25%)** for that shot.
  - **Platform safe zone:** keep everything above the bottom ~20% (Reels/TikTok UI and
    descriptions) and below the top ~10%, with ~8% side margins.
- **Check by hand:** after auto-captioning (CapCut, Premiere, Submagic, Captions app), go
  through every talking-head shot and confirm no caption touches my face. Drag any that do.

---

## 5. Reframing my footage to 9:16

- **Talking head:** crop to center on me, with my face in the top half and my chest left free for captions.
- **Horizontal B-roll:** reframe to 9:16 keeping the main action or face centered. Use an
  auto-reframe/face-tracking pass, then fix by hand. Use the crop room to animate slow
  pans that follow the action (this doubles as the constant drift).
- **Screenshots:** fit to width on a white background, then animate the zoom toward the key detail.
