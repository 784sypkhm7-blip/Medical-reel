# Medical Case Reel – "It smelled funny while I was welding…" style

Recreation prompt built from a frame-by-frame breakdown of the 68-second reference video
(1280×720, 16:9, 24 fps). It copies the **structure, pacing, shot types, color grade,
motion graphics and caption style**. The narration is original (it doesn't copy the source
script). Brand names are generic placeholders so you don't reuse someone else's trademarks.

---

## 1. Master prompt (paste this into your AI video tool or give it to your editor)

> Create a 68-second, 16:9 (1280×720 or 1920×1080), 24 fps cinematic medical-storytelling reel
> in the style of a doctor explaining a real ER case. Two visual worlds intercut:
>
> **A) THE DOCTOR (me):** medium close-up, chest-up, centered, eye-level. Navy blazer over a
> white shirt with a tiny dot print, open collar. Background: warm beige/grey office wall with
> soft window-blind light streaks and a framed abstract painting (red/blue) on the right,
> heavily blurred (f/1.8 look). Warm, soft key light from camera-left, gentle fill, no hard
> shadows. Expressive delivery with hand gestures: raised index finger ("one thing…"),
> hand to chest ("couldn't breathe"), shrug. Slow, constant 3–5% push-in on every
> doctor shot.
>
> **B) THE PATIENT STORY (cinematic B-roll):** a curly-haired man in his 20s in a
> metal-fabrication workshop. Warm teal-and-orange grade, shallow depth of field, 50–85 mm
> look, slow handheld drift or slow dolly. Every shot slowly moves: push-in, slide or
> rack-focus. Never fully static.
>
> Tone: suspenseful, conversational, a "medical mystery" told like a story. Hard cuts on
> the beat, each shot 1.5–6 s, with sound-design hits (whoosh, sizzle, grinder whine, low
> drone) under the voiceover. Low ambient tension music bed at -24 LUFS under the voice.

## 3. Motion-graphics spec

1. **Quote word-pop (hook):** text in quotation marks (e.g. `"I CAN'T BREATHE"`), font
   *Bebas Neue / Anton / Oswald Bold*, white `#FFFFFF`, 3 px black stroke + soft 40% drop
   shadow. Animation: scale 80%→100% + opacity 0→100% over 3 frames (ease-out), hold, cut off
   on the word. In the reference it sits **on the chest, under the collar**, never over the face.
2. **Cyan smoke VFX (shot 3):** stock smoke element, tinted `#2FD3E0`, Screen/Add blend at 70%,
   rising from the bottom edge, plus orange `#FF9A3C` spark particles with motion blur.
3. **Arc-flash glow (shots 10, 14):** Glow/Bloom (threshold 70%, radius 60 px, cyan tint), plus
   2 white flash frames at the arc strike and a 4–6 px random-position shake for 8 frames.
4. **Screenshot slide-in (shot 5):** 8-frame ease-out slide from the right, then a continuous
   100%→108% Ken-Burns zoom toward the price. Optional red hand-drawn circle (stroke draw-on, 10 frames).
5. **Punch-in jump zooms** on doctor emphasis lines: instant 100%→112% scale on the cut.
6. **Constant drift:** every clip has a 3–8% scale or position keyframe across its duration.
7. **Pointer line (end):** 3 px red `#E53935` line, draw-on from subject to object, 6 frames.
8. **Grade:** warm teal-and-orange, lifted blacks slightly, skin protected. Patient-in-hospital
   shot cooler and desaturated. Light film grain at 5%.

## 4. Captions that **never cover your face**

Burned-in, word-by-word captions for the whole video.

- **Style:** Montserrat ExtraBold or Bebas Neue, 2–4 words per line, ONE line at a time, white
  with a 3 px black stroke. Highlight the key word (e.g. *phosgene*, *can't breathe*) in yellow `#FFD400`.
- **Animation:** each word pops in on its spoken syllable (scale 90%→100%, 2 frames).
- **Placement rules (most important):**
  - **Doctor shots (your face):** caption baseline at **78–85% of frame height**, so it sits on
    your chest/blazer, well below your chin. If your chin ever drops below ~65% of the frame
    height, push the caption to the very bottom (90%). Keep a 10% safe margin from the edges.
  - **B-roll shots:** bottom-center at ~85% height. If a face or key action is at the bottom
    (e.g. shot 2 hospital bed), move the caption to the **top-third (12–18% height)**.
  - **Never** place captions in the region from your eyebrows to your chin. Use face-tracking
    (CapCut "Auto captions" + manual drag, Premiere "Auto Reframe"/face tracking, or Submagic /
    Captions app with "avoid face") and check every doctor shot by hand.
- **Vertical (9:16) version:** if you also export for Reels/TikTok, keep captions at **60–70%
  height** (above the platform UI), and frame yourself so your face sits in the top 40%.

## 5. Production notes for filming your doctor shots

- Camera at eye level, 50–85 mm, f/1.8–2.8, 1.5–2 m from a background with a framed print and
  window-blind light. Frame head + chest, leaving the chest area free for captions.
- Wear a navy blazer over a light patterned shirt, open collar.
- Record the hook, the "here's the detail that matters" line and the end question as separate
  takes so you can cut them in as punch-ins.
- B-roll: shoot or license welding, grinding, spark and hospital shots, or generate them with
  the per-shot descriptions in §2 (each row works as a standalone text-to-video prompt: add
  "cinematic, shallow depth of field, warm teal-orange grade, 24 fps, slow camera drift").
